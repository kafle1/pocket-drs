Pocket DRS turns one phone into a ball-tracking camera for cricket practice.

Put the phone on a tripod behind the bowler's stumps, mark both sets of stumps once, and press Ball (or a volume key) after each delivery. For every ball you get:

• where it pitched
• where it hit the pad
• where it would have gone on to the stumps, drawn over your video and in 3D
• the speed, when the clip shows enough of the ball to measure it
• an LBW verdict: out, not out, or umpire's call, with the reason

How sure it is
Every number comes with how far off it could be. When the clip can't support a call, the verdict is umpire's call. When the tracked ball doesn't make sense, or the path is too unsure to call, you get no verdict and a short note on what to fix, never a guess.

What you need
• A tripod or anything that holds the phone still, a couple of metres behind the bowler's stumps
• Both sets of stumps in the frame
• A red or pink ball
• The main camera lens, not the ultra-wide
• An internet connection. The video is analysed on a server and a result takes a few seconds.

Short net? Set the real pitch length in Settings.

Also works on clips you already have: Analyse a video, trim to the delivery, mark the stumps, send.

Privacy
No accounts, no ads, no analytics. The server deletes your video as soon as it has analysed it, and the results after a day.

It is a coaching tool, not an umpire. It can't tell bat from pad, so a ball that hit the bat first isn't out whatever the verdict says.

Pocket DRS is open source under the AGPL-3.0.
